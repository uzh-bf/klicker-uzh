## I.I.D. Returns and a Random Walk

They are related but are not the same object. I.i.d. describes the innovations: they are independent over time and have the same distribution. A random walk describes the price process generated by accumulating innovations, for example in log prices:

$$
\ln P_t=\ln P_{t-1}+\delta+\varepsilon_t,
$$

where $\delta$ is an optional drift and $\varepsilon_t$ is the new innovation. Thus, i.i.d. innovations can underpin a random-walk price model, but a return increment is not the price level. Independence alone also does not imply that the innovations are normally distributed.

A random walk does not mean that price formation is irrational. In the EMH interpretation used in the course, price changes are unpredictable because predictable information should already be reflected in the current price and new information arrives unpredictably.

If today's return innovation is independent of yesterday's, what information from yesterday could still help predict today's innovation?
