## Expectations and Two-Fund Separation

Under homogeneous expectations, all investors agree on expected returns, variances, and covariances. They therefore identify the same tangency portfolio. Differences in risk aversion change only how much each investor combines that common risky portfolio with the risk-free asset. This produces two-fund separation.

Under heterogeneous expectations, investors disagree about expected returns and may therefore identify different optimal risky portfolios. Their risky holdings are no longer generally proportional to one common tangency or market portfolio, so the classical two-fund-separation result fails.
