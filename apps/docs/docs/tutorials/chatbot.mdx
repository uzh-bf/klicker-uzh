:::info Beta feature
Course chatbots are AI beta features. Beta features are enabled by default. You can disable or re-enable them in [User Settings](/getting_started/beta_features/). Lecturers can create and configure drafts; publication requires approval before students can use them.
:::

## What is the Course Chatbot?

The course chatbot is an AI assistant for a specific course. It can use a connected knowledge base, offer several learning modes, and limit usage through a participant credit policy.

:::warning
AI-generated answers can be wrong or incomplete. Review answers and source references against the official course materials before relying on them.
:::

## How do I create and publish a course chatbot?

1. Open **Resources → Chatbots** and select **Create chatbot**. Enter a **Name**, an optional **Description**, and the **Course**, then select **Create draft and continue**. The linked course is fixed after creation.
2. In **Chatbot basics**, review the identity and save your changes. In **Learning modes**, choose **Tutor**, **Explainer**, or **Quizzer**, add a **Chatbot framing** if useful, and select **Save learning modes**. Keep **Tutor** or **Explainer** enabled.
3. Open the prepared knowledge base. Expand **Connected chatbots**, choose the chatbot from **Chatbot**, and select **Connect chatbot**. A chatbot can use one knowledge base at a time; if another one is connected, the action is **Replace knowledge base**. Connect only resources that have a current version and are **Available to AI**.
4. Return to the chatbot and open **Knowledge**. Under **Knowledge graph**, choose whether to enable **Show the participant concept map** and **Use graph connections for document search**, then select **Save knowledge graph settings**. The connected knowledge graph must be published first. These settings are staged with the revision and apply after approval; graph connections provide additional search hints while answers still refer to document passages.
5. Complete the **Participant disclaimer** by entering its title and introduction, then save it. A complete disclaimer is required before requesting publication.
6. In **Model & Reasoning Settings**, choose whether to **Let participants choose the model**. If disabled, choose the **Selected model** and **Reasoning effort**. If enabled, choose the **Allowed models** and permitted reasoning efforts, then select **Save model settings**. Review any configured tools shown under **Usage** and change them only when the page provides a control.
7. Under **Participant credits**, set **Initial credits**, **Reset period**, **Reset amount**, and **Max credits**, then save the participant credit settings. A credit policy is required for publication.
8. Open **Review and submit**, provide the **Use case** and **Expected student count**, and select **Request publication**. The request is reviewed before publication. A saved draft or pending revision is not active; only a revision with **Published** status is active. If a request is rejected, make the requested changes and select **Resubmit for approval**.

## How do I verify the published chatbot?

After the revision is published, select **Open owner preview** and test the chatbot before sharing it:

1. Ask a question whose answer is clearly supported by the connected knowledge base. Check the answer and its source references against the original material.
2. Ask a follow-up question that depends on the first question. Confirm that the conversation keeps its context.
3. Ask an unrelated or unsupported question. Confirm that the chatbot communicates its limits instead of inventing an answer.
4. Reload the preview and confirm that the published chatbot and its conversation remain available.

Keep reviewing generated answers and sources as you use the chatbot. Finish this AI workflow after the published chatbot gives a supported answer with source references, handles a follow-up appropriately, and responds safely to an unsupported question.
